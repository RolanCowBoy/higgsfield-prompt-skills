# Diagnostic patterns

Use these as reasoning examples, not fixed settings. They are authored adaptations from the PASSPORT RUSH prompt analysis (source record numbers below); rendered success was not evaluated.

## Classify defects versus risks
- Record 7 combines an unbroken take with four hard-cut segments. This is a textual conflict, not merely a likely model failure. Choose the intended mode or present alternatives if both are user requirements.
- Records 5/6 put two detached handles in the character's hands before the second break. Repair the state sequence, not the emotional adjectives.
- Record 59 assigns thousands of words to four seconds. Length alone is not proof of failure. The stronger findings are too many readable obligations and an important driver gaze before the camera can see it.
- Record 84 transfers surface, light and color while retaining construction. Do not flag the changed shadow color as unwanted geometry change.

## State repair
Bad sequence:
1. Right handle detaches and stays in right hand.
2. Both detached handles are held.
3. Left mount breaks.

Coherent sequence:
1. Right handle detaches and stays in right hand.
2. Left hand grips the still-mounted left handle; right retains the detached one.
3. Left mount breaks; each hand retains its own handle.

Preserve the user's cause of failure. “She pulls both handles off deliberately” would be a different action.

## Time and visibility repair
If settings request 5 seconds but beats total 9, report the conflict. If only supporting micro-gestures are optional, condense those; if every event is essential, propose a longer clip or separate shots. Label any changed setting.

If a reaction must be seen before a mirror tilts toward its subject, either revise the initial framing to include the subject or move the reaction to a visible interval. Check whether the user locked framing or event order before choosing.

## Editing conflict
“Change only the hair; keep the face and scene unchanged” is feasible as an instruction, with necessary hair shadows and occlusion allowed.
“Double the head size; preserve every outline exactly” is incompatible. Preserve identity and the rest of the scene, not the superseded silhouette.

## Minimal useful response
Confirmed: supplied duration and timeline disagree.
Risk: two full prop interactions plus a facial turn may not read at this scale.
Revision: one self-contained prompt with settings consistent with the user's choice.
Next check: both hands and the prop stay visible at the decisive contact.

Do not add speculative failures for every anatomy, logo, or material if they are irrelevant to the request.
