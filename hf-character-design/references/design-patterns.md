# Character design patterns

Authored examples informed by PASSPORT RUSH records 24–26, 63–66, 76–77. These demonstrate prompt construction, not proven generation results.

## Describe form before decoration
A useful structure for a fluffy animal:
“One compact rounded body, almost no visible neck, short supporting legs, and a tail large enough to balance the silhouette. Small ears and clearly separated eyes remain readable through the fur.”

The shape relationship gives more control than “cute, beautiful, ultra-detailed fluffy animal.” Choose a medium and rendering treatment separately.

## Express a mischievous personality in a still
For a newly designed child, if the user has supplied the relevant age/style:
“An oversized tousled black curl mass rises away from the forehead, leaving both brows visible. The head is large but balanced by the torso. Two small front teeth show in a restrained crooked smile. He holds a cloth puppet behind his back, shoulders turned slightly away while his eyes check someone just outside the frame.”

This is an illustrative design direction, not an assertion about an unseen image. Preserve actual approved anchors if a reference is supplied. Do not automatically add a puppet, two teeth, or black curls to other characters.

## Controlled alternatives
For three variants of one role:
- keep costume, age, palette, and attitude common;
- vary a single dominant axis: compact round silhouette / long narrow silhouette / broad upper body with short legs;
- describe how the resulting personality reads;
- once selected, freeze those design anchors for downstream sheets.

Avoid varying face, palette, species, clothing, and medium simultaneously unless the user explicitly wants broad exploration.

## Clothing layers
For a cloth hand puppet, preserve its usable construction: a hollow fabric body with an open lower sleeve for the wearer's hand and a soft puppet head. Choose whether it is worn or held according to the requested pose. Do not replace it with a stuffed rag doll with an unrelated body construction.

Describe visible layers in construction order: inner shirt, outer cardigan, neckline/cuffs/hem, trousers or skirt, footwear, accessories. Mention only features relevant at the image's scale.
Check that a “fully zipped jacket” does not also expose an entire chest design underneath.

## Cast composition
For a group, choose a hierarchy of heights, rows, overlapping silhouettes and visible faces. Preserve exact requested counts as a criterion to inspect, not a guarantee. A supplied cast reference governs existing identities; descriptive diversity is for characters not yet specified.
